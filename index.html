1. Obstructive Sleep Apnea Risk Prediction:
● Project description: Using a cohort of 265,000 patients from Kaiser Permanente Southern California, I
developed machine learning prediction models using electronic health record (EHR) data to assess
obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) risk. These models significantly outperformed existing, labor-intensive
screening paradigms. External validation at KUMC confirmed their sustained performance and
transportability. Furthermore, I applied transfer learning techniques, including stacking and warm-start
fine-tuning, to enhance performance. These models are now being piloted in primary care and
preoperative workflows across multiple Kaiser Permanente regions.
● Techniques used/What I learned: Robust data cleaning and external dataset harmonization, nested cross
validation pipeline for estimating performance, Bayesian Optimization for tuning parameters, decision
curve analysis for evaluating clinical utility, DeLong test and 95% bootstrapped confidence intervals to
compare performance and determine statistical significance, Shapley values for feature importance,
transfer learning techniques (warm-start fine-tuning and stacking), Bayesian framework to update model
predicted probabilities.
● Outputs:
○ First-author manuscript Hwang NH, et al. Machine Learning Models Using Electronic Health
Record Data to Predict Obstructive Sleep Apnea. Currently under peer-review with the American
Journal of Respiratory and Critical Care Medicine (Impact Factor: 21.7)
○ First-author preprint manuscript at medRxiv: https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.05.26364806
○ Three first-author abstract publications at medical conferences (SLEEP 2026, CHEST 2026, and
Internal Medicine Research 2026)

2. PromptNLP–a novel natural language processing technique:
● Project description: To support the curation of structured data for BDSP and a planned target trial
emulation, I sought to extract apnea-hypopnea index (AHI) and Epworth Sleepiness Scale (ESS) values from
unstructured clinical notes. Through the course of this effort, I developed PromptNLP, a novel two-phase
process that uses large language models to generate accurate, hallucination-free regular expression
(regex)-based data extraction. PromptNLP was internally validated at Kaiser Permanente and externally
validated at BIDMC and Emory University, demonstrating cross-site generalizability. The process has
extracted AHI and ESS values for more than 176,000 patients, enabling large-scale sleep research. I am
now expanding PromptNLP by developing a preprocessing “denoising” layer that clusters relevant phrases
and removes irrelevant note sections before Phase 1. I am also designing a structured agentic pipeline to
create a complete end-to-end notes-to-results workflow.
● Techniques used/What I learned: Prompt engineering, regular expression, two-proportion z-test, and the
Pydantic AI agent framework.
● Outputs:

3. Obstructive Sleep Apnea Phenome Wide Association Study:
● Project description: I created a clinical phenome-wide atlas of obstructive sleep apnea (OSA) using EHR
and sleep-study data from over 220,000 adults. I performed both prevalence and incidence PheWAS
analyses to evaluate associations between OSA and a comprehensive range of EHR-derived domains,
including diagnoses (PheDAS), laboratory values (LabWAS), medications (MedWAS), procedures
(ProcWAS), cardiorespiratory measurements (CRMWAS), healthcare utilization (UtilWAS), health behaviors
(BehWAS), and patient-reported symptoms (QWAS). The atlas revealed especially strong and consistent
cardiometabolic associations across domains, with comparatively weaker associations for several
mental-health and cognitive outcomes. I also developed subdomain clusters and cross-linked them to
identify 8 novel multidimensional OSA phenotypes that integrated physiologic, symptomatic, and
comorbidity patterns and predicted select outcomes beyond conventional AHI-based severity metrics. The
resulting atlas was designed to serve as a centralized, hypothesis-generating resource for OSA researchers
through supplements and an interactive web portal.
● Techniques used/What I learned: large scale multivariable analyses (selecting covariates, creating
Manhattan/Volcano plots), PheWAS/GWAS frameworks, inverse probability weighting (IPW), survival
analyses (e.g. Cox regression), Bonferroni/FDR measures of significance, large scale data harmonization,
probabilistic principal component analysis (PPCA), K-Means clustering, Latent Class Analysis, and immortal
time bias mitigation.
● Outputs:
○ First-author manuscript Hwang NH et al. A Clinical Phenome-Wide Atlas of Obstructive Sleep
Apnea: Cross-Sectional Associations and Longitudinal Incidence Across the Electronic Health
Record under peer-review at Nature Medicine (impact factor: 52.5).
○ Website link: https://www.osaatlas.com/
4. Predicting Long-Term Continuous Positive Airway Pressure (CPAP) Therapy Adherence (Behavioral
Phenotyping)
● Project description: I conducted an epidemiologic and machine learning study of long-term CPAP
adherence using EHR, sleep-study, patient-reported questionnaire, and remotely-transmitted CPAP data. I
first identified baseline demographic, clinical, behavioral, and sleep-related factors associated with
one-year adherence. I then developed XGBoost and logistic-regression models to predict one-year
adherence from pre-treatment data and evaluated models incorporating early CPAP use. Early usage data
substantially improved prediction compared with baseline characteristics alone. Finally, I used longitudinal
modeling to identify four distinct one-year CPAP-use trajectories and developed machine learning models
to predict these patterns from baseline and first-week treatment data.
● Techniques used/What I learned: Robust data cleaning and external dataset harmonization, nested cross
validation pipeline for estimating performance, Bayesian Optimization for tuning parameters, decision
curve analysis for evaluating clinical utility, DeLong test and 95% bootstrapped confidence intervals to
compare performance and determine statistical significance, Shapley values for feature importance, KNN
imputation for missing data, logistic regression for multivariate analyses, longitudinal clustering
(group-based trajectory modeling [GBTM], latent class growth analysis), and manuscript
development/submission.
● Outputs:
○ First-author manuscript Hwang NH et al. Predicting One-Year CPAP Adherence and Use
Trajectories Utilizing Baseline and Early Use Data under peer-review at SLEEP (impact factor: 7.0)
○ First-author preprint manuscript at medRxiv: https://doi.org/10.64898/2026.10.01.26363483
○ First-author abstract pending peer-review at SLEEP 2027 and American Thoracic Society
International Conference 2027.
○ Models are being integrated into a digital population management platform to identify and
prioritize high-risk patients for early follow-up, leveraging SHAP values to tailor personalized
interventions.
5. Evaluating the Use of a Titratable Pre-Fabricated Mandibular Advancement Device to Predict Custom Oral
Appliance Therapy Response
● Project description: Oral appliance (OA) therapy is underutilized largely because clinicians cannot reliably
predict who will respond, particularly in severe OSA. I retrospectively analyzed data from a clinical pilot at
Kaiser Permanente in which patients being considered for OA therapy were first trialed with a titratable
pre-fabricated OA. Pre-fabricated OA responders demonstrated a positive predictive value of 93.4% for
responding to a custom OA across the full range of OSA severities. The results support clinical utility of
functional phenotyping to identify candidates for OA therapy.
● Techniques used/What I learned: Retrospective study design with real-world clinical pilot data, evaluation
of selection and attrition bias.
○ Outputs: First-author manuscript Hwang NH et al., Evaluating the Use of a Titratable
Pre-Fabricated Mandibular Advancement Device to Predict Response to Custom Oral Appliance
Therapy. Accepted for publication at Journal of Dental Sleep Medicine (issue assignment
pending).
6. Target Trial Emulation of CPAP Therapy on Cardiovascular Outcomes
● Project description: I am starting a new project conducting a target trial emulation of the SAVE trial (CPAP
therapy for cardiovascular prevention in obstructive sleep apnea; McEvoy et al., NEJM 2016) using EHR
data from Kaiser Permanente. Emulating this randomized trial from observational data applies causal
inference methods to estimate the effect of CPAP on rates of acute cardiovascular events in patients with
OSA.
● Techniques used/What I learned: Target trial emulation framework: propensity-score/IPTW to emulate
randomization, clone-censor-weight for per-protocol effects, and time-to-event modeling.
7. ResMed Internship (Current)
● Project description: I am applying digital signal processing (DSP) to raw multi-axis accelerometry data
from smartphones to derive cardiac and respiratory motion metrics for physiologic phenotyping. I utilized
Fourier Transforms (via NumPy’s FFT) to calculate respiratory rates and applied 0.5–20 Hz bandpass filters
to the z-axis to isolate electrocardiogram signals.
● Techniques used/What I learned: Fourier Analysis (FFT), filters, signal types, Hilbert Transform, Cepstrum
Analysis, and other DSP techniques.

project to phenotype sleep apnea.
